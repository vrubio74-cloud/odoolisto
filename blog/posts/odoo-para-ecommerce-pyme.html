Yet that's exactly what happens to thousands of SMEs: the store runs on one side (Shopify, WooCommerce, PrestaShop) and the ERP or accountant runs on the other, and every new order means copying data by hand, eyeballing stock levels, and waiting until month-end to know if the business is doing well. ¿Por qué una pyme necesita una tienda online integrada con su ERP?

Why does an SME need an online store integrated with its ERP?

Cuando la tienda online y la gestión del negocio viven en sistemas distintos, aparecen los mismos problemas una y otra vez: stock que se vende por duplicado porque la web no sabe lo que ya se vendió en tienda física, facturas que hay que generar a mano copiando cada pedido, y un CRM que solo ve la mitad de tus clientes porque los de la tienda online no aparecen en ningún sitio.

When the online store and the rest of the business run on different systems, the same problems show up again and again: stock sold twice because the website doesn't know what already sold in the physical shop, invoices that have to be generated by hand by copying each order, and a CRM that only sees half your customers because the ones from the online store never show up anywhere.

Con Odoo, la tienda online no es una isla: comparte la misma base de datos que Ventas, Inventario, Compras y Contabilidad. Eso significa que el stock, los precios y las fichas de producto se gestionan una sola vez y se reflejan automáticamente tanto en la web como en el punto de venta físico, si lo tienes.

With Odoo, the online store isn't an island: it shares the same database as Sales, Inventory, Purchasing and Accounting. That means stock, prices and product pages are managed once and automatically reflected both on the website and at the physical point of sale, if you have one.
